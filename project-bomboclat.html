01Why a game

A new cohort is a room full of strangers. We turned the awkward part into a game.

The problem

Social friction.

At the start of an Apple Developer Academy cohort, learners find it hard to break the ice, start real conversations, or get to know each other beyond polite professional introductions.

The idea

A digital icebreaker.

An offline, local multiplayer game you play face to face — handing the phone back and forth in a high-stakes duel. The shared tension of the ticking bomb, and the pixel explosion that follows, do the rest.

03The manual

Simple rules. Sweaty palms.

01

The hidden fuse

Every round, the timer lands somewhere between 10 and 15 seconds — and nobody ever knows exactly when it will go off.

02

No spamming

A cooldown on the Pass button stops anyone mashing it. Passing becomes a question of timing and nerve.

03

1P or 2P

Practise against the CPU, or play the real thing: two people, one phone, face to face.

04

A cast you know

Custom pixel avatars of Academy members, the ArcadeClassic font, and animated GIF reactions for every close call.

04Under the hood

A state machine in a pixel coat.

Game logic lives in one view model — who holds the bomb, whose button is cooling down, who just lost a heart — so SwiftUI and SpriteKit only ever draw what it decides.

ready→ holding⇄ passed · cooldown→ exploded→ game over
ArchitectureMVVM — timers, health and turns kept apart from the UI.
Game logicGameLogicViewModel runs the state machine: ownership, cooldowns, win and loss.
InterfaceSwiftUI for navigation, onboarding and the HUD.
SceneSpriteKit (an SKScene inside SwiftUI via SpriteView) for the background and particles.
DataSwiftData and UserDefaults (TrophyManager) for wins and unlockable characters.
MediaAVFoundation for music and effects; animated GIFs through a WKWebView wrapper.

From the first idea to TestFlight — the concept, the flows, and the game’s main SwiftUI views.

  5. 05

    Shipping

    Managed the lifecycle from ideation to TestFlight deployment.
